AI practice

AI that fits the way work actually runs.

A practical guide to enterprise AI: start with a real workflow, ground the model in business data, and keep people in the decisions that matter.

What travels well

Four ideas worth carrying into enterprise work.

01

Workflow

Design a workflow, not a chatbot.

The strongest cases connect input, interpretation, action, and follow-up. The model is one part of a process with an owner, an output, and a next step.

02

Data

Make business data usable first.

Standard fields, shared definitions, clean records, and a dependable knowledge base matter more than a clever prompt. Data readiness is part of the AI product.

03

Control

Automate the repeatable; keep judgment visible.

AI can handle retrieval, classification, drafting, and rule-based checks. High-impact decisions, exceptions, and uncertain outputs still need a named human owner.

04

Adoption

Let domain experts shape the system.

The people closest to the work know the edge cases, the language, and the quality bar. Their participation turns AI from an imposed tool into a useful teammate.

Enterprise examples

What these patterns make concrete.

01

Recruiting

Close the loop from first message to human decision.

An AI recruiting workflow can configure role goals, screen candidates, communicate, schedule interviews, write back to the system, and hand off at the moments that need a person.

24/7 coverage and 70-80% interview attendance when human reminders stay in the flow.

Enterprise lesson: measure the whole journey, not one automated message.

02

Finance

Standardize the source before automating the books.

A finance team first unified its source table and field definitions, then used AI, formulas, code, and RPA to compare, map, and route the work. The automation was built around a clearer data model.

Invoice preparation moved from 3 people over 3 days to 1 person for half a day; sensitive fields were masked and outputs were checked.

Enterprise lesson: automation becomes safer when the input contract is explicit.

03

Legal

Move from document review to process visibility.

The legal case followed a durable sequence: put contracts online, standardize reusable clauses, then use AI for first-pass review and drafting. Workflow timing and bottlenecks became visible alongside the text.

AI reduced repetitive review work while legal review remained mandatory; process data made cycle time and late signing easier to manage.

Enterprise lesson: the lasting value is often the operating model around the model.

04

CRM

Start with the customer record people can actually use.

A lightweight CRM combined a shared customer record, interaction history, voice-to-field capture, reminders, dashboards, and personalized follow-up. The design favored familiarity and adoption over technical novelty.

70-80% of routine work could be delegated, while the final customer touch and data checks stayed with people.

Enterprise lesson: the best AI layer is often the one users do not have to learn from scratch.

05

Manufacturing

Turn expert judgment into a feedback loop.

The quality-inspection case connected consistent image capture, a labeled defect library, on-site inference, expert review, and continued model improvement. Frontline specialists became coaches rather than spectators.

A shift from sampling to full inspection, with throughput near 50,000 parts per hour and accuracy around 95%.

Enterprise lesson: explainability and expert participation are adoption infrastructure.

06

Decision support

Build a data hub before a multi-agent layer.

The water-management case connected multiple data sources, a domain knowledge base, small specialist workflows, and a routing layer. The architecture made it easier to know which tool was responsible for which question.

Routine reporting and analysis accelerated, while high-impact warnings and public-safety decisions stayed under human review.

Enterprise lesson: higher autonomy needs stronger routing, provenance, and escalation rules.

A delivery pattern

A sequence you can use on a real program.

  1. 01

    Name the work

    Choose one painful, frequent workflow with a clear owner and a visible business outcome.

  2. 02

    Define the input

    Agree on fields, source systems, permissions, context, and what counts as a good result.

  3. 03

    Shape the handoffs

    Break the work into small steps and decide what the model may do, suggest, or never decide.

  4. 04

    Measure the outcome

    Track cycle time, quality, adoption, exceptions, rework, and business impact - not speed alone.

  5. 05

    Learn in the flow

    Use real exceptions, user feedback, and expert review to improve the workflow one iteration at a time.

A useful filter

What not to copy blindly.

01

Do not start with

A giant AI program

A small, high-frequency use case gives the organization evidence and trust before the scope expands.

02

Do not assume

The model fixes bad data

Inconsistent records, unclear ownership, and scattered knowledge will show up as unreliable outputs.

03

Do not hide

The human checkpoint

Make review, escalation, auditability, and responsibility explicit wherever the cost of error is high.

Career Prep Platform

The demo started with a simple question: how can an AI workflow turn scattered career information into preparation that feels structured, useful, and still owned by the person using it?

A product-style AI prototype that turns a job description, resume, and target role into a guided interview-prep workflow.

FormatProduct prototype · temporarily offlineScopePrototype workflow · structured input · multi-step AI flow

Outcome: A documented experiment in prompt design, user flow, limits, deployment, and iteration.

Project detailsPublic link paused while the next version is being prepared.
01
The setting

From scattered input to a useful next step

A job seeker may have a job description, resume, target company, role context, and personal notes spread across different places. The challenge was not generating more text; it was creating a guided path from raw material to something a person could actually practice.

02
The tension

Useful without pretending

The workflow had to be useful without pretending to know more than the input supported. It also had to be simple enough to try, controlled enough to operate publicly, and clear enough that a user could see where AI helped and where human judgment remained necessary.

03
The design principle

A workflow, not a chatbot

The product value comes from the sequence of tasks and decisions around the model, not only from the model's ability to produce fluent text.

Workflow in view

Make the transformation visible.

  1. 01

    Start with the user's material

    The workflow accepts the job description, target role, company context, and resume information that a person already has, then organizes it around the preparation goal.

  2. 02

    Make the transformation visible

    Instead of hiding the prompt work behind one button, the experience gives the user a sequence: understand the target, identify likely themes, generate practice, and review the result.

  3. 03

    Bound the useful answer

    The output is shaped around interview preparation rather than unlimited content generation, which makes it easier to review, refine, and compare with the source material.

  4. 04

    Ship, observe, and iterate

    Public deployment, usage limits, user flow, and future provider integration are treated as part of the project—not as details that begin after the demo is finished.
