Public sector · Dynamics 365 cloud migration

Modernizing a provincial CRM for cloud operations

A transformation program shaped around continuity, resilience, and a roadmap for future Dynamics 365 capabilities.

Project story

The difficult part was not moving a CRM. It was modernizing a service platform without asking frontline teams to carry the risk.

The setting

A long-running provincial public-sector CRM supported more than 500 users and years of case and service knowledge. Frontline staff, service managers, delivery teams, and release stakeholders all depended on the same platform, but they experienced its risks from different angles.

Why now

The organization needed a more resilient cloud foundation while continuing to serve people through an operating model that could not simply be paused or replaced overnight.

The tension

Every modernization decision had to improve the platform for future Dynamics 365 and Power Platform capabilities while protecting continuity, data confidence, and user trust.

People in the system

Every project has more than one point of view.

Perspective

Frontline service teams

A dependable system that remains familiar when a case is moving and a person is waiting for help.

Perspective

Program and service managers

Visibility, predictable release decisions, and confidence that the change will not interrupt service.

Perspective

Business and delivery teams

A shared language for process gaps, scope, acceptance criteria, and readiness.

Perspective

Release and support teams

A controlled path from testing to go-live, with clear ownership when something does not behave as expected.

The journey

How the work moved from problem to practice.

  1. 01

    Start with service reality

    Discovery began with the work people actually performed: the case journeys, handoffs, data dependencies, and moments where the existing platform slowed or obscured a decision.

  2. 02

    Turn risk into a roadmap

    The program separated continuity-critical work from future capability work, so modernization could move forward without turning every improvement into a single high-risk release.

  3. 03

    Test the moments that matter

    Business validation focused on real service journeys and the information users needed to act, not only on whether the platform technically opened or a deployment completed.

  4. 04

    Release with a safety net

    Readiness, UAT, stakeholder coordination, and go/no-go decisions created a shared checkpoint before the new operating foundation became the team's daily environment.

Decisions that shaped it

The turning points were business decisions as much as system decisions.

Decision

Continuity before novelty

The first measure of success was protecting service continuity. New capability mattered, but only after the core service experience was stable enough to trust.

Decision

Business readiness is different from platform readiness

A technically successful migration was not treated as a complete business outcome. Process owners still needed to validate the journeys, data, roles, and practical change.

Decision

Make go/no-go evidence-based

The release conversation was anchored in test evidence, known gaps, ownership, and recovery options rather than optimism or a calendar date.

Ways to read the work

The same project can reveal different kinds of value.

Business lens

Protect the service promise

The project is a story about continuity, public trust, and preserving the knowledge already embedded in a long-running service operation.

Delivery lens

Make change navigable

The value came from translating gaps into scope, acceptance criteria, UAT, readiness conversations, and a release path that people could follow.

Systems lens

Create a foundation that can evolve

The new platform direction created room for future Dynamics 365 and Power Platform capability without treating the migration as an isolated technical event.

What changed

Outcomes are easier to trust when the evidence is visible.

Scale

More than 500 users and long-lived case and service data made continuity and institutional knowledge central to the design.

Delivery evidence

Discovery, functional delivery, UAT, release readiness, and stakeholder coordination connected the business need to the deployment decision.

Business outcome

A smoother go-live, faster page loads, and a more stable foundation for future Dynamics 365 and Power Platform improvements.

Reflection

A migration becomes meaningful when the people who depend on the system can feel the improvement without having to carry the implementation risk themselves.
