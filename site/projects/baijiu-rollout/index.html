Consolidating systems for a national consumer brand

An enterprise program to reduce fragmentation and create a shared view across the value chain.

Project story

At national scale, the challenge was not one CRM or one ERP feature. It was making channels, customers, warehouses, campaigns, and operations behave like one business.

The setting

A top-three Chinese baijiu group was growing across production bases, distributor networks, e-commerce channels, customer operations, finance, and data. The operating picture included thousands of distributors, millions of online customers, multiple distribution centers, and a business that could not afford to treat every channel as a separate world.

Why now

Growth had created overlapping systems, fragmented ownership, and inconsistent visibility across production, distribution, customer relationships, marketing, finance, and supply-chain activity.

The tension

The organization needed enterprise consistency without flattening the differences between B2B distribution, B2C commerce, customer insight, warehouse fulfillment, and equipment operations. Speed mattered, but so did inventory control, consent, traceability, and recoverability.

People in the system

Every project has more than one point of view.

Distributor and channel teams

Reliable order, price, credit, availability, and fulfillment information for a wide network of partners.

E-commerce, marketing, and customer service

A usable customer view across channels, with consent and campaign context that could support better decisions.

Warehouse and fulfillment teams

Clear allocation, routing, reservation, batch, shipment, and exception handling across distribution centers.

Finance, asset, and technology teams

Stable system boundaries, auditable data, controlled integration, and a platform that could be supported after go-live.

The journey

How the work moved from problem to practice.

  1. 01

    Create one enterprise picture

    The program brought the business landscape into one conversation: customer and channel operations, Customer Insights, F&O, Power BI, warehouse fulfillment, finance, and equipment-related work.

  2. 02

    Make the order path resilient

    B2B, B2C, and external-channel demand needed a common intake, validation, routing, reservation, status, and reconciliation story instead of a different interpretation in every channel.

  3. 03

    Turn customer data into a decision asset

    Identity, consent, segmentation, activation, and campaign results needed to remain connected so marketing could act on a customer view that was explainable rather than merely large.

  4. 04

    Connect fulfillment to the real operation

    Warehouse availability, safety stock, reservation, batch genealogy, shipment status, and exceptions had to stay grounded in the system of record rather than being solved by manual inventory edits.

  5. 05

    Design for adoption at scale

    UAT, role-based training, phased rollout, operational support, and hypercare connected the enterprise design to the people who had to use it every day.

Decisions that shaped it

The turning points were business decisions as much as system decisions.

Keep source-of-truth boundaries visible

Customer context, customer insight, inventory, fulfillment, finance, and analytics each needed a clear responsibility so integration did not become a second hidden database.

Treat recovery as part of the customer experience

Duplicate orders, delayed messages, reservation failures, and status disagreements needed idempotency, correlation, reconciliation, and controlled replay so the business could recover without guessing.

Use AI and automation as governed assistance

Recommendations could accelerate matching, segmentation, service, and exception handling, but high-impact identity, consent, inventory, budget, and release decisions still needed ownership and review.
